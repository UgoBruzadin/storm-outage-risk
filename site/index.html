Trap 1

No record doesn't mean no outage.

The federal tracker only logs counties that have customers out. When the tracker itself goes quiet, every county looks fine. So the pipeline checks the whole country's pulse first: if the tracker was down, the county's outage is unknown, not zero.

Trap 2

The easy way to look smart.

Most outages last for hours, so "it's out now, it'll still be out" scores very well. That's a real forecast, of how long outages last, but it answers a different question from "which calm county goes dark next?", and mixing the two inflates the score fourfold. Everything here is scored on new outages only.

Score: PR-AUC, how well real outages are ranked ahead of false alarms. Random guessing scores the outage rate itself, about 0.005.

What it's worth · an estimate for 2025

Worth millions where the official forecasts lose money.

Suppose a utility had sent repair crews to wait wherever each forecast pointed. Crews cost money every time, and pay off when the power goes out, because it comes back sooner. Each dot is one scenario.

Net savings: what outages cost homes and businesses (Berkeley Lab's outage-cost models) that staged crews avoid, minus the cost of staging. Scenarios: crews shorten outages by 10% or 25%; staging costs $5,000 or $20,000 per county per 6 hours. Every forecast stages by the same rule, using 2020–2024 data only. Best case: the model sees each period's weather as it happened.
